J. Homework 1(b) — one step, g(x,y) = xy^2 - x^3 exp(x-y) + sin^2(xy)
==============================================================================
  derivative check g_x at (1,1)                             rel. err 5.21e-11  OK
  derivative check g_y at (1,1)                             rel. err 1.32e-11  OK
  d/dx = y^2 - (3x^2 + x^3) e^(x-y) + 2 sin(xy)cos(xy) * y
  d/dy = 2xy + x^3 e^(x-y) + 2 sin(xy)cos(xy) * x
  at (1,1): grad = (-2.090703, 3.909297)
  sin(2) = 0.909297 is the term that makes both numbers irrational
  one step with alpha = 1:  (x,y) = (1 - -2.090703, 1 - 3.909297) = (3.090703, -2.909297)
  f(1,1) = 0.708073   f(3.0907,-2.9093) = -11884.398381
  the value went DOWN by 11,885.1: the direction is a
  descent, but alpha = 1 is enormous - the step overshoots far past any minimum.

==============================================================================
  Model 1: yhat=[5, 7, 9, 2, 12]
           errors=[0, 0, 0, -1, 2]  |e| sum=3  e^2 sum=5
           MAE = 0.6000   MSE = 1.0000
  Model 2: yhat=[4.5, 7.5, 10, 2.5, 11]
           errors=[-0.5, 0.5, 1, -0.5, 1]  |e| sum=3.5  e^2 sum=2.75
           MAE = 0.7000   MSE = 0.5500
  VERDICT: the two metrics disagree. Model 1 wins on MAE (0.6 vs 0.7),
  Model 2 wins on MSE (0.55 vs 1.00) because Model 1's single 2-unit miss
  is squared, while Model 2 spreads five small misses. 'Which is better'
  has no answer until the loss is chosen - and MSE punishes the outlier.
