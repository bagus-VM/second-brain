# Flashcards — Exercise Sheet 6

## Flashcards

> [!question]- What is the E-I index and how do you interpret it?
> [!answer]- The E-I index = (E − I)/(E + I), where E = external (cross-group) edges and I = internal (within-group) edges. It ranges from −1 (pure homophily: all ties within group) to +1 (pure heterophily: all ties across groups). A value near 0 means ties form randomly regardless of group membership.

> [!question]- What is the difference between selection, socialization, and confounding in social networks?
> [!answer]- Selection: similar people become friends (birds of a feather). Socialization: friends become similar over time (influence). Confounding: a third factor (e.g., same dormitory) causes both friendship and similarity. Selection and socialization produce the same cross-sectional pattern — only longitudinal or experimental data can separate them.

> [!question]- What are the three types of closure in affiliation networks?
> [!answer]- (a) Triadic closure: two people connected to a third become connected (A–C and B–C → A–B). (b) Focal closure: two people who share an affiliation become connected (both attend same event). (c) Membership closure: a person joins a new group because a friend is already in it.

> [!question]- What is Schelling's segregation model and why is it surprising?
> [!answer]- Agents on a grid prefer at least k of 8 neighbours to be the same type; dissatisfied agents move. It is surprising because mild individual preferences (k = 3 or 4 out of 8) can produce strong global segregation — far more extreme than anyone individually desired. The network in Schelling's model is the spatial grid of neighbours.

> [!question]- Why can't cross-sectional data alone distinguish selection from socialization?
> [!answer]- Both mechanisms produce the same observable pattern: friends are similar. Cross-sectional data is a single snapshot — you cannot see whether similarity preceded friendship (selection) or followed it (socialization). You need longitudinal data (to track changes over time) or experimental data (to randomly assign friendships) to separate the mechanisms.
