## One-line Summary
Electronics is the scientific field concerned with manipulating electrons and other electrically charged particles using devices built from physical principles.

## Core Intuition
Think of electricity like water flow — electronics is the engineering discipline that builds "valves" and "pipes" to control how electrons move. Just as plumbing controls water to do useful work, electronics controls charge to process information and deliver power.

## Formal Definition / Statement
Electronics is a subfield of physics and engineering that applies the principles of electromagnetism to design circuits and devices that control the flow of electrons and other charge carriers for practical purposes (computation, communication, signal processing, power conversion).

## Key Properties / Complexity
- Rooted in [[electricity]] — the presence and motion of electric charge
- Subfields include [[microelectronics]] (micrometre scale) and [[nanoelectronics]] (nanometre scale, quantum effects)
- Devices operate using [[semiconductor]] materials (primarily [[silicon]])
- Binary states (conducting/non-conducting) map to Boolean algebra — the foundation of digital computing
- Systems with >2 states enable unconventional computing (e.g., ternary logic)

## Worked Example
A simple electronic system: a digital logic gate built from [[mosfet]] transistors. By combining nMOS and pMOS transistors in a CMOS configuration, we create gates (NOT, NAND, NOR) that perform Boolean operations — the building blocks of all digital processors.

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusing electronics with electrical engineering — electronics focuses on small-signal and information-processing circuits, while electrical engineering encompasses power systems, machines, etc.
- Assuming all electronics is digital — analog electronics (amplifiers, filters) remains critical
