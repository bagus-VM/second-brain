## One-line Summary
The valence band is the highest energy band occupied by electrons in a non-excited atom, representing the outermost bound electron states.

## Core Intuition
Think of the valence band as the "home base" for electrons — these are the electrons in the outermost orbitals that are still bound to their atoms. For current to flow, electrons must be excited out of this band into the [[conduction-band]].

## Formal Definition / Statement
The valence band is the range of energy levels corresponding to the outermost occupied electron orbitals in a non-excited atom. For [[silicon]], these are the 3s and 3p orbital electrons. Electrons in the valence band can be excited to the [[conduction-band]] by gaining energy equal to or greater than the [[bandgap]], leaving behind [[electron-hole]] that act as positive charge carriers.

## Key Properties / Complexity
- Contains electrons in the outermost occupied orbitals
- For Si: 3s² 3p² electrons (4 valence electrons)
- Electrons can be excited to the [[conduction-band]]
- When an electron leaves, it creates an [[electron-hole]] — a positive carrier
- The energy difference to the [[conduction-band]] is the [[bandgap]]

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusing valence electrons with all electrons — only outermost shell electrons are in the valence band
- Forgetting that holes in the valence band are mobile charge carriers

## Connections
