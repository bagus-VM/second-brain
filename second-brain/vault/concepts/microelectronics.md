## One-line Summary
Microelectronics is the subfield of electronics dealing with very small electronic components at the micrometre scale or smaller, built on semiconductor materials.

## Core Intuition
If electronics is plumbing, microelectronics is the miniaturization of that plumbing into the size of a grain of sand. Components are so small that millions of transistors fit on a single chip — this is what makes modern computers, phones, and embedded systems possible.

## Formal Definition / Statement
Microelectronics is the subfield of [[electronics]] that focuses on the design, fabrication, and application of electronic components with dimensions on the micrometre (μm) scale or smaller. It primarily uses [[semiconductor]] materials, especially [[silicon]], to create integrated circuits containing transistors, diodes, resistors, and capacitors.

## Key Properties / Complexity
- Component dimensions: micrometre (10⁻⁶ m) scale or smaller
- Built on [[semiconductor]] wafers (primarily [[silicon]])
- Fabrication processes include [[photolithography]], [[ion-implantation]], and [[thermal-diffusion]]
- Enables integration of billions of components on a single chip (VLSI)
- Binary states from semiconductor switches map to digital logic

## Worked Example
A modern processor chip contains billions of [[mosfet]] transistors, each with gate lengths of ~5-7 nm. This is microelectronics in practice — each transistor acts as a switch controlled by electric fields, and collectively they implement complex digital logic.

## Common Pitfalls
- Confusing microelectronics with nanoelectronics — microelectronics deals with classical device behaviour at micrometre scale; [[nanoelectronics]] exploits quantum mechanical effects at nanometre scale
- Assuming microelectronics only means digital circuits — analog microelectronics (sensors, RF, power management) is equally important

## Connections
- [[electronics]] — parent field
- [[nanoelectronics]] — subfield at even smaller scales
- [[semiconductor]] — material foundation
- [[silicon]] — dominant semiconductor material
- [[mosfet]] — fundamental component
- [[photolithography]] — key fabrication technique

## Open Questions
- As feature sizes shrink below 3 nm, when does microelectronics become nanoelectronics?
- What new materials (e.g., GaN, SiC, graphene) will complement or replace silicon?
