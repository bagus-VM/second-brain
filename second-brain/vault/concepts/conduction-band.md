## One-line Summary
The conduction band is the energy band above the valence band where excited electrons become free to carry electrical current.

## Core Intuition
If the [[valence-band]] is "home base" for bound electrons, the conduction band is the "free roaming zone." Electrons that gain enough energy to jump the [[bandgap]] enter this band and can move freely through the material — this is electrical conduction.

## Formal Definition / Statement
The conduction band is the range of energy levels that excited or freed electrons can occupy, containing real or hypothetical orbitals of excited electrons. For [[silicon]], these correspond to 4s, 3d, or 4p orbitals. Electrons in the conduction band are delocalized and free to move under an applied electric field, carrying current.

## Key Properties / Complexity
- Contains free (delocalized) electrons that carry current
- For Si: 4s, 3d, or 4p orbitals
- Separated from [[valence-band]] by the [[bandgap]]
- In conductors, conduction band overlaps with valence band (no gap)
- Number of electrons in conduction band determines conductivity
- In [[n-type-semiconductor]], donor atoms provide extra electrons to conduction band

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming conduction band electrons are "free" in the vacuum sense — they move through the crystal lattice
- Forgetting that both conduction band electrons and valence band holes contribute to current

## Connections
- [[valence-band]] — source of excited electrons
- [[bandgap]] — energy barrier between valence and conduction bands
- [[semiconductor]] — small bandgap allows controlled excitation
- [[n-type-semiconductor]] — donor atoms populate conduction band
- [[intrinsic-semiconductor]] — thermal excitation creates conduction electrons
